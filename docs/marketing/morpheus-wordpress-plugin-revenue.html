Morpheus WordPress Plugin — Revenue at Scale

What revenue the platform produces as the WordPress plugin scales from 1,000 to 1,000,000 active users — built from the repo's own business model, with the assumptions and caveats called out rather than smoothed over.

Prepared 15 October 2026 · Source: the repo's own business-model documents and the internal audit at docs/audits/morpheus-business-model-digest.md · Figures are projections resting on an assumed usage profile, not measured billing data.

Read this first — what "active user" means here

Every figure below treats an active user as a paying Morpheus account that uses the WordPress plugin (connected site, consuming AI actions). It is not a raw plugin install. Installs become revenue only through an install → paying conversion rate, and — as the repo's own audit states plainly — no telemetry exists yet to measure that rate. So: read these numbers as "revenue at N paying active users", not "revenue at N downloads".

The unit: one active user, annualized

The model prices every action in credits (1,000 credits = $5 retail) and bills at a flat 2× markup over DeepSeek's rate, so profit on usage is roughly equal to the underlying AI cost. One "realistic active user" costs Morpheus about $19.10/yr in AI and retails at about $38.55–$40.35/yr.

The $19.10/$20.18 cost and $38.55/$40.35 retail are the repo's BR-v6 / FIN figures, resting on the assumed "120 code-producing turns + 14 chats + 3 diagnoses + 3 compiles per month" profile. That profile is assumed, not measured — see the caveats.

From markup to something closer to net

The headline figures deduct no infrastructure, monitoring, Stripe fees, domain, analytics or storage. The repo's operations plan puts a "fixed floor" under each tier — the minimum monthly burn before those variable lines. Subtracting just that floor (and nothing else) gives a truer picture:

Active usersFixed floor / moFixed floor / yrMarkup − fixed floor / yr
1,000~$86~$1,032~$18,400
10,000~$176~$2,112~$192,500
100,000~$701~$8,412~$1,942,000

The 1,000-user row is the fragile one. The repo's operations plan states the assumed engagement at 1,000 users may not cover even the ~$86/month floor — the break-even needs ~430% of the assumed average paid engagement. Treat 1,000 users as the tier where the model is least proven, not a small version of the million-user tier.

Assumptions and caveats — the honest part

  • The usage profile is assumed, not measured. $19.10/yr and $38.55/yr come from "120 build turns + 14 chats + 3 diagnoses + 3 compiles per month" — the repo's own operations plan labels every such profile "a stated assumption, not measured data". There is no telemetry confirming real users behave this way.
  • That profile is the app builder's, not a WordPress-site owner's. 120 build turns/month describes someone building software. A WordPress operator may do far fewer AI-heavy actions, so per-user revenue could be lower than $38.55/yr — the internal audit even records a 40-turns-vs-120-turns conflict in the source documents, so the true WordPress ARPU is genuinely unknown. Read the tables as the model's ceiling until real WordPress usage is measured.
  • "Profit" is markup, not net. The $19,457…$19.46M figures exclude infrastructure, monitoring, Stripe fees, domain, analytics and storage. They are gross profit on usage, not bottom-line profit.
  • "Active user" ≠ install. Revenue = paying active users × ARPU. Raw plugin installs must first be multiplied by an install→paying conversion rate that is unmeasured.
  • The source documents disagree with each other. The repo's own audit (docs/audits/morpheus-business-model-digest.md) catalogued 27 internal inconsistencies — free-tier wording, per-user cost ($1.591 vs <$1 vs $2.47/mo), Priority Compile pricing, and the fixed-floor arithmetic. The foundations (2× markup, $0.80/$2.40 DeepSeek rate) are consistent; the reporting layer is not. This sheet uses the BR-v6 figures as the single baseline and flags the rest rather than averaging incompatible numbers.

The bottom line

As a linear, pay-as-you-go model, revenue scales simply: 1,000 users ≈ $38.5k/yr → 10,000 ≈ $385k → 100,000 ≈ $3.86M → 1,000,000 ≈ $38.55M of retail revenue, with usage-markup gross profit of roughly $19.5k → $195k → $1.95M → $19.46M before fixed and infrastructure costs. The honest caveat is the mirror image of the clean curve: those numbers ride on an assumed per-user usage profile and an unmeasured install-to-paying conversion, and the WordPress-specific profile may be lighter than the builder profile they were calibrated against. The model is sound in shape; the level of each tier is the thing to validate with real billing data before quoting it to anyone as a fact.
